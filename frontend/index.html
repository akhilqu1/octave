How Octave Works

Keep playing the songs you love

Hand pain shouldn't force you to put your guitar down. Whether your joints ache or certain chords feel out of reach, Octave finds simpler ways to play the same music.

01 / Easier shapes Skip the barre chords

Play full chords without barring strings

Pressing your index finger flat across all six strings takes a lot of thumb and wrist strength. Octave swaps those stiff barre grips for open shapes that are much easier to hold.

Chords like F major and B minor are tough for a reason. To make every string ring clearly, you have to clamp your index finger down hard and bend your wrist at a sharp angle. If your hands get sore or stiff, holding that grip for a whole song hurts.

Octave replaces full barres with open strings and simple three- or four-finger shapes. Instead of barring all six strings for F major, you can fret four middle strings and let the high E string ring open. You get the same chord sound with a fraction of the grip force.

02 / Smooth changes Whole-song planning

Move smoothly from one chord to the next

An easy chord isn't much help if your hand has to jump five frets to reach it. Octave looks at all the chords in your song together so your fingers barely have to move between changes.

Most chord charts show you one chord at a time. When you put them together in a song, your hand often has to slide up and down the neck or lift every finger at once.

Octave compares all the ways you can play each chord and picks the path with the least movement across the whole song. Whenever two chords share a note, Octave keeps that finger planted on the string as an anchor while your other fingers step to the next shape.

03 / Fit to your hands Hand size and capo tools

Adjust chords for your reach and capo

Frets near the headstock are spaced far apart, which can be a tough stretch for smaller or stiffer hands. Octave adjusts chord shapes to match your reach and shows you where a capo can make a song easier.

You can set how many frets you want to stretch across, turn off barre chords, or avoid using your pinky finger. Octave filters out any shapes that don't fit your settings.

If a song is in a tricky key with lots of sharps or flats, Octave also checks frets 1 through 7 to see if a capo helps. Putting a capo on fret 1, for instance, lets you play a song in A♭ major using simple open G, C, and D shapes.
